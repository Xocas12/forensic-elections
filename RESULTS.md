## Anchor 1 — excess mass at integer percentages

Kobak, Shpilkin and Pshenichnikov (2016), *AOAS* 10(1), 54-73. Under the null each station's numerator is redrawn as `Binomial(denominator, observed share)`, so the comparison is against the rounding that honest arithmetic produces at that station's size. A station counts as "on an integer" within 0.05 percentage points. Before counting, every station whose turnout or winner's share is over 99% is excluded, as the paper does (section 3.1): 100% is always an integer, so without that rule the null predicts more hits at 100 than honest data produce and the excess comes out biased low. Stations with a denominator below 100 are then excluded as well. Both counts are shown.

Every row also carries the **honest-data bias**: the mean excess the same procedure reports when each station's numerators are redrawn as binomial noise around the shares the data report, on the real denominators, so that there is nothing to find (20 replicates, 50 null draws each, mean ± standard error). A calibrated test gives a bias near zero; whatever is left is the calibration error that remains, printed next to the number it would distort.

### The size control (known trap 1)

Small stations land on whole percentages honestly: with 200 voters every attainable turnout is a multiple of 0.5 points. A pooled national statistic is therefore not a result until it is shown to survive size conditioning. If the excess were an arithmetic artefact it would be **largest** in the smallest band and vanish in the largest. ## Anchor 3 — a second mode in the turnout distribution

Klimek, Yegorov, Hanel and Thurner (2012), *PNAS* 109(41), 16469-16473, with their exclusion rule (electorate below 100). The paper asserts a second mode but supplies no test for one, so the test is named here rather than inherited: Silverman's critical-bandwidth statistic (1981), the smallest Gaussian bandwidth at which the kernel density of turnout still has one mode, against a smoothed bootstrap.

| election | critical bandwidth (pp) | p | stations | excluded |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2011 Duma | 6.45 | 0.0010* | 91,082 | 4,143 |
| 2018 presidential | 5.71 | 0.0010* | 93,601 | 4,098 |

Unimodality is rejected in both. `p` cannot go below 1/(999+1) by construction, and it is at that floor.

### Where the modes are

Located with a 2-point bandwidth, which is deliberately wider than the one-point period of the sawtooth anchor 1 just established: a narrower kernel resolves the sawtooth's teeth and reports them as a row of modes.

In both elections there is a principal mode at ordinary turnout and a second mode within two points of complete turnout, and the winner's share at the near-100 per cent mode is far above the winner's share at the principal one. That is the anchor's claim, and it is the joint location, not the bimodality, that carries it.

**Known trap 2 applies and is not resolved here.** Bimodality alone is not evidence of falsification: urban and rural heterogeneity, and republics with different mobilisation patterns, produce a second mode in honest elections too. What the table above adds is *where* the second mode sits and what the winner scores there. Separating that from genuine heterogeneity needs the covariates this repository has not acquired, and is not claimed.

## Anchor 2 — the comet tail: not run

`elections.analysis.comet_tail` still raises `NotImplementedError`. The Shpilkin tail estimator has not been read from a primary source, and the published anomalous-vote counts for 2011 and 2018 are still marked TO CONFIRM in `docs/validation_anchors.md`. A plausible-looking tail estimator written from memory would produce a number with nothing to check it against, which is the failure this project is organised to avoid. It is left raising, with the reason in the exception message.

## What is not claimed

- **No claim that any particular election was falsified.** These are distributional anomalies against a specific null. They are consistent with the published literature and they are not, on their own, proof about any individual polling station.
- **The false-positive control is outstanding.** Anchor 1 item 3 asks for the excess to vanish on the Poland 2010 and Spain 2011 control tables. Those tables are not in `data/raw`, so the control has not been run. Until it is, the size-band trend above is the internal evidence that the test is not merely detecting arithmetic.
- **Anchor 3 does not separate falsification from heterogeneity**, for the reason in trap 2.
